## Candidate theory

Humor may be modeled as a response occurring near the boundary of an established order: a deviation must be sufficiently unexpected to disrupt expectation while remaining sufficiently interpretable to permit recognition, reframing, or resolution.

## Conceptual model

```text
World Knowledge
  -> Order
  -> Premise
  -> Expectation
  -> Expected / Actual
  -> Deviation
  -> Interpretability
  -> Resolution
  -> Humor
```

## Theoretical distinction

```text
Unexpected
  !=
Funny
```

The theory predicts a potentially non-linear relationship between deviation and humor: insufficient deviation may be unremarkable, intermediate deviation may produce humor, and excessive deviation may become incomprehensible.

## Candidate theoretical contribution

The proposed contribution is an operational bridge between world knowledge, expectation, measurable deviation, and human humor judgment.

This must be tested against prior theories and baselines. It is not claimed as established fact.

## Testable predictions

1. Human judgments of humor should not be explained by deviation magnitude alone.
2. Interpretability should moderate the relationship between deviation and humor.
3. A structured deviation representation should provide explanatory value beyond simple semantic distance.
4. Controlled manipulation of deviation should reveal a boundary or region associated with increased humor judgments.

## Falsification criteria

The theory should be rejected or substantially revised if these predictions repeatedly fail under controlled experiments or if simpler models explain the observations equally well.
